About

Conversational AI often replaces human thinking—a process known as cognitive offloading. While efficient, this risks eroding our mind.

We propose a shift to Cognitive Augmentation: designing Conversational AI that scaffolds human thinking to foster learning, agency, and long-term personal growth.

Demonstration
Demonstration of LLM-based In-situ Thought Exchanges for Critical Paper Reading
Xinrui Fang, Koji Yatani · The University of Tokyo

Reading academic papers requires an active critical thinking process. Most existing reading augmentation tools focus on efficiency, risking cognitive offloading. This demonstration presents features that foster critical thinking over AI reliance, using multiple AI agents to scaffold rather than replace the reader's own reasoning.

Poster
Do Health Management Chatbots Replace Our Health Decision-making?
Weiran Cui, Chi-Lan Yang, Takuji Narumi · The University of Tokyo

A qualitative study examining how chatbot roles (expert, coach, partner, influencer) shape users' responses to health advice. Findings show users in health contexts actively monitored, cross-checked, and evaluated recommendations independently, suggesting health AI may support rather than replace decision-making.

Design Concept
Layered AI Skills as Cognitive Support for Growth and Direction in Teams and Organizations
Simo Hosio · University of Oulu

ContextGolem explores structured, multi-layered AI context to support higher-order functions such as reflective coaching, goal alignment, and critical self-evaluation. Configurable skill architectures adapt to different contexts and roles, keeping the human in control while enabling progressively more relevant cognitive support.

Participation

We invite submissions that identify a core problem or propose a solution related to the risks of cognitive offloading in conversational AI.

• Dependency and reduced self-reflection
• Erosion of critical thinking
• Over-automation of emotional reasoning
• Design strategies for long-term growth

We welcome research, design, artistic, and practice-based contributions. Accepted contributions will be presented in short.

Submission
  • Paper or short essay
  • Poster
  • Video
  • Interactive prototype
  • Speculative design
